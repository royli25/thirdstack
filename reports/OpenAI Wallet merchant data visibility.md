# ChatGPT Wallet hands merchants tokens, not chains

**No. A Nike checkout snippet built like Flovia would find no on-chain wallet and no other transactions to enrich from an OpenAI wallet payment, because there is nothing on-chain to find.** The product the user calls "OpenAI Wallet" is **ChatGPT Wallet**, launched November 13, 2026. It is a vault of tokenized **credit cards** that OpenAI's "dots" agents use to check out on merchants' own websites. It holds no balance, no stablecoins, no crypto and no blockchain addresses, and OpenAI says it does not hold customer funds. Nike receives what it gets from any card-not-present web order: the buyer's name, contact details and shipping address, plus a **network token** in place of the card number, and possibly a Payment Account Reference (PAR) that ties the token to the underlying card account. Nothing found suggests OpenAI passes merchants a persistent OpenAI user ID or the buyer's purchases at other merchants. On-chain payer analytics works only on rails where the payer's address is visible and belongs to one person: x402, MPP on Tempo, and payments from embedded or smart wallets. It reveals almost nothing when the payment comes from an exchange's pooled hot wallet. In the EU, linking any such address to a named customer makes the address and its history regulated personal data. For a founder building an MCP router like Two Hands, the takeaway is that the biggest agent wallet now in production produces no on-chain identity at all. The agent-payments world is splitting into card-token rails, where the agent platform controls identity, and crypto rails, where identity leaks by default. A payer-analytics pivot should be judged on that split, not on OpenAI's wallet.

A caveat on sources: the researchers could not load OpenAI's, Stripe's or the EDPB's pages directly. Every finding comes from search-engine snippets and secondary coverage of a product that is one week old, so exact contract wording, field names and the tokenization program in use remain unverified.

## ChatGPT Wallet is an Apple Pay-style card vault with no ledger behind it

OpenAI announced the product as "Introducing ChatGPT Wallet." Access starts with **US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and over who hold eligible US-issued credit cards**, and rollout is gradual ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/chatgpts-new-wallet-lets-ai-agents-shop-and-check-out-for-you/); [Fintech Magazine](https://fintechmagazine.com/news/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-for-secure-ai-agent-payments)). It runs inside **dots**, the always-on personal agents OpenAI launched at DevDay on September 29, 2026. Each dot has its own cloud computer and browser ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/29/openai-launches-dots-always-on-ai-agents-in-chatgpt-with-their-own-cloud-computers/)). Debit cards are excluded. OpenAI states it "does not hold customer funds or provide banking services through ChatGPT Wallet" ([Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/chatgpt/articles/openais-chatgpt-wallet-lets-ai-154218980.html); [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/11/16/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-for-ai-agent-purchases/)). Decrypt says outright that the wallet **"does not hold crypto or a cash balance"** and uses no USDC or other stablecoin ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383278/openai-chatgpt-wallet-ai-agents-shop-without-crypto)). OpenAI has not said whether stablecoins will be added later ([KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-for-ai-agent-purchases-excludes-cryptocurrency)).

A purchase works like this. The dot finds the item and assembles the order. The user reviews the items, total and card, then **approves every purchase with Face ID or another biometric**, so agents cannot spend on their own today ([Fintech Magazine](https://fintechmagazine.com/news/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-for-secure-ai-agent-payments); [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/330799/20261115/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-face-id-approval-sole-human-check-ai-agent-card-payments.htm)). Once the user approves, the card network generates a **payment token**: a substitute card number with its own security code and expiry date. The dot types it into the merchant's ordinary checkout form ([Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/chatgpt/articles/openais-chatgpt-wallet-lets-ai-154218980.html); [Blockonomi](https://blockonomi.com/chatgpt-wallet-lets-ai-agents-pay-without-ever-seeing-your-card-number/)). Merchants need **no wallet-specific integration**, and the wallet will not work on sites that block agents ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/chatgpt-wallet)). OpenAI plans to let users pre-approve spending within limits, categories or time windows, but has given no date ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/chatgpt-is-ready-to-reach-into-your-wallet-2000839784)).

Custody is simple: there is none in the crypto sense. OpenAI receives **"a tokenized credential and limited card information"** (network, issuer, card type, last four) and never the full card number or security code ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/openai-chatgpt-wallet-agent-payments-launch-2026); [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/chatgpt-wallet/)). There are no per-user addresses and no pooled omnibus account. Money moves from the user's card issuer to the merchant's acquirer through normal card clearing, and **OpenAI is not in the flow of funds**. The partners are the card networks: **American Express, Mastercard and Visa**, with Chase named in OpenAI's post but left out by some outlets. OpenAI also works with **Adyen, Checkout.com and Stripe** on payments and with **Forter, Riskified, Sardine and Signifyd** on fraud, so that merchant systems accept legitimate agent orders instead of flagging them ([Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/11/16/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-for-ai-agent-purchases/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/13/openai-chatgpt-wallet/)). Coverage disagrees on whether the tokens run through Visa Intelligent Commerce and Mastercard Agent Pay or whether OpenAI simply has not said ([Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/chatgpt-wallet/)).

This also marks a change of direction. OpenAI's 2025 Instant Checkout ran checkout inside the chat over the Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP) and Stripe's Shared Payment Tokens. OpenAI scaled it back around March 2026, saying the first version "did not offer the level of flexibility that we aspire to provide" ([Webinterpret](https://webinterpret.com/en/blog/openai-abandons-instant-checkout)). ChatGPT Wallet goes the other way. The agent drives the merchant's own site with a browser, and OpenAI's Commerce Partner Playbook asks merchants to accept **Web Bot Auth** (signed agent requests) at their CDN or firewall and to **tag agent orders** ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/11/16/openai-chatgpt-wallet-for-ai-purchases-through-retailer-checkouts/); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/ai/chatgpt-wallet-commerce-partner-playbook-merchants/)).

## Nike gets an ordinary card order, and a Flovia snippet would capture no address

From Nike's side, a ChatGPT Wallet purchase is a **card-not-present web checkout completed by a browser agent paying with a network token**. OpenAI's help article says a merchant "may need your name, phone number, email, or billing or delivery address to complete checkout" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001654-making-purchases-with-chatgpt-wallet)). Press coverage citing OpenAI describes the dot handing over only the shipping and contact details the order requires ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/chatgpts-new-wallet-lets-ai-agents-shop-and-check-out-for-you/)). Settlement is ordinary fiat through Nike's acquirer. There is **no blockchain address, no transaction hash and no on-chain leg** for a snippet to read ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383278/openai-chatgpt-wallet-ai-agents-shop-without-crypto)).

A Flovia-style wrapper on Nike's checkout would therefore capture three things. It would get the order identity, which Nike already collects. It would get the card token, which is useless for enrichment and contractually off-limits for reuse: OpenAI's playbook reportedly says **"the customer consents only to the specific transaction"** and "don't reuse or save that payment credential for a separate purchase" ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/ai/chatgpt-wallet-commerce-partner-playbook-merchants/); [The Keyword](https://www.thekeyword.co/news/openai-commerce-partner-playbook)). And it would get signals that the order came from an agent. The one persistent payment-side identifier is the **PAR**. Merchants can use it to associate a Wallet token with the underlying card account, which lets Nike recognize a returning card across tokens ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/openai-hands-its-ai-agents-a-wallet-without-the-real-card-number-in-it/)). PAR is an EMVCo standard that the industry already uses for loyalty and analytics, and Visa offers a PAR lookup API ([Visa developer](https://developer.visa.com/capabilities/visa-par-inquiry)). It links one card within Nike's own data. It does not give access to that card's activity at other merchants.

OpenAI keeps the cross-merchant view. It retains purchase details, approval and rejection records, authentication results and the request sent to each merchant, and it reportedly says it does not share or sell this data for targeted advertising ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/openai-chatgpt-wallet-agent-payments-launch-2026); [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/chatgpt/articles/openais-chatgpt-wallet-lets-ai-154218980.html)). The researchers found **no evidence that OpenAI passes merchants a buyer's other purchases, conversation content or an OpenAI user ID**. That conclusion rests on secondary summaries and the absence of contrary reports, because the Wallet Privacy Notice itself could not be read.

The older ACP path shows how close the line sits. There, the merchant's server got a `buyer` object (first name, last name, email, optional phone) and a `fulfillment_address` ([agenticcommerce.dev](https://www.agenticcommerce.dev/docs/reference/checkout)). On Stripe, the seller's granted Shared Payment Token exposed card `last4`, billing details and a **card `fingerprint`**, a stable per-card identifier that supports repeat-buyer matching inside the seller's Stripe account ([Stripe API](https://docs.stripe.com/api/shared-payment/granted-token/retrieve)). Stripe computes fingerprints from the device or token number for Apple Pay- and Google Pay-style credentials rather than from the real card number ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/how-do-card-numbers-work-with-apple-pay-and-google-pay-and-what-is-dynamic-last4)). For network-tokenized Wallet payments, then, the **email address and PAR, not the card fingerprint, are the strongest identifiers for linking repeat buyers**. Under every OpenAI flow, the merchant sees about as much identity as a normal web order provides and nothing beyond it.

## Wallet architecture decides whether an address reveals a person

The question of what Nike could see becomes interesting only on stablecoin rails, and there the answer depends on who controls the sending address. **Exchanges send from pooled hot wallets.** Coinbase's user agreement lets it hold customer assets in shared omnibus addresses without creating one per user ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/coinbase/coinbase-user-agreement/coinbase-may-use-omnibus-blockchain-addresses/)). Stripe's documentation confirms the effect from the receiving side: refunds go to the sending address, and **"exchange or omnibus wallet payers might not be able to recover refunded funds"** ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/deposit-mode-stablecoin-payments)). Elliptic puts the limit plainly: **"attribution to an exchange identifies the service, not the person using it"**, because the mapping to individual customers lives in the custodian's off-chain ledger ([Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/wallet-attribution.html); [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3524/blockchain-privacy/wallet-clustering-limits.html)). Enriching an omnibus payment yields "this came from Coinbase" and a transaction history belonging to millions of other users.

**Embedded and smart wallets are the opposite.** Coinbase Smart Wallet derives one deterministic address that is **identical across 248 chains** ([GitHub: coinbase/smart-wallet](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/coinbase/smart-wallet/main/README.md)). Privy splits keys inside a TEE but gives each user a persistent wallet, and its documented "privacy" features concern key custody, not transaction shielding ([Privy](https://docs.privy.io/security/security-faqs)). Paymasters and bundlers change who pays gas and who submits the transaction, so **`tx.origin` is a shared bundler address, not the payer** ([CDP](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/paymaster/need-to-knows/account-abstraction-basics)). A snippet that reads only `tx.from` would mislabel thousands of users as one entity. A correctly built one reads the token `Transfer` event or the UserOperation `sender`, recovers the user's smart-account address, and can rebuild that user's history on every EVM chain. Bridge (Stripe) says it uses **"individual segregated wallets per customer rather than pooled wallets"**, but it is unclear whether that means a distinct on-chain address per customer or segregation in Bridge's own ledger ([Bridge API docs](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/docs/custodial-wallets)).

| Payment source reaching the merchant | What a payer-analytics snippet can capture | Does enrichment reveal the individual? |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT Wallet (card network token) | Order PII, token, brand/last4, possibly PAR; no address | No on-chain data exists |
| ACP / Stripe SPT (card) | `buyer` PII, `last4`, card fingerprint inside the seller's Stripe account | No on-chain data exists |
| Exchange or fintech send (Coinbase, likely PayPal) | Exchange hot-wallet address | Only the platform; history is the exchange's ([Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/wallet-attribution.html)) |
| Stripe Pay with Crypto / deposit mode | USD settlement, but the charge's `payment_method_details.crypto` includes the buyer wallet address and tx hash ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/deposit-mode-stablecoin-payments)) | Depends on the sender: omnibus reveals the platform, self-custody reveals the person |
| Embedded / smart wallet (Coinbase Smart Wallet, Privy) | Persistent per-user address, the same across chains | Yes, a full pseudonymous cross-chain history |
| Tempo mainnet | Public EVM sender | Yes; Tempo Zones hide in-zone transfers, but deposits reveal the sender ([Tempo](https://tempo.xyz/blog/introducing-tempo-zones/)) |
| x402 | `payer` address and tx hash in the settlement record ([Coinbase CDP](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/support/faq)) | Yes, if the agent wallet is per-user |
| MPP (Tempo session / via SPT) | Channel-funding address; no address when paid by card SPT ([mpp.dev](https://mpp.dev/payment-methods/tempo/session); [Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine/mpp.md)) | Yes on Tempo; no on the card path |

Three things in this table matter for a payer-analytics product. First, **Stripe already gives merchants the payer's sending address** through its API, so a front-end snippet adds little for address capture. Stripe's hosted crypto.link.com page means the snippet may never see the wallet connection anyway ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/crypto/stablecoin-payments.md)). Any value lies in enrichment. Second, **x402 suits automatic capture best**: the payer address arrives in the payment response with no wallet-connect step ([Coinbase CDP](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/api-reference/v2/rest-api/x402-facilitator/settle-payment)). MPP sessions cut on-chain activity to one open and one close, but the payer still funds the channel from a visible address ([Tempo](https://tempo.xyz/blog/mpp-sessions)). Third, **privacy tooling is barely in production**. Apart from Tempo Zones, which give the zone operator full visibility, the payer-privacy designs for x402 are hackathon prototypes such as Private-Escrow x402 ([ETHGlobal](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/private-escrow-x402-fj0v1)).

Clustering does not close the gap for pooled custody. The deposit-address heuristic links Ethereum users who reuse the same exchange deposit address ([Springer, FC 2020](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-3-030-51280-4_33)), but it yields **pseudonymous clusters, not names**. The step that actually de-anonymizes a payer is the one the merchant takes: joining the address to the order's name, email and shipping address. There is one subtle leak. A buyer who withdraws from an exchange to a self-custody wallet and then pays exposes the exchange withdrawal in that wallet's history, revealing which exchange they use, roughly how much and when.

## Linking an address to an order makes it regulated personal data

The legal picture has hardened. The EDPB adopted **Guidelines 02/2025 on blockchain, version 2.0, on July 7, 2026**. They state that a wallet address "qualifies as personal data under Article 4 GDPR when it can be associated with an identified or identifiable natural person". They also say on-chain metadata, including transaction identifiers and event logs, can be personal data too ([EDPB Guidelines v2](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_202502_blockchain_v2_en.pdf)). The board **rejected consultation requests to exclude wallet addresses** from the definition ([EDPB consultation report](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpbreport_publicconsultaton_guidelines_blockchain_202502_en.pdf)). A merchant that attaches an address to a named order has made it identifiable by definition, along with every transaction it pulls in. In California, the CCPA covers "unique personal identifiers" and any information "reasonably capable of being associated with" a consumer ([Cal. Civ. Code 1798.140](https://www.consumerprivacyact.com/section-1798-140-definitions/)). The Attorney General's 2022 opinion held that **inferences drawn entirely from public information must still be disclosed**, because the inference is itself personal information ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-ag-issues-decision-on-disclosure-of-inference-data-in-response-to-ccpa-access-requests)).

Enrichment from public sources already has enforcement history. The UK ICO found that Experian engaged in "invisible processing" of publicly available data. On appeal, the tribunal overturned much of the enforcement notice but rejected Experian's argument that notifying people would take disproportionate effort ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/media2/migrated/2618467/experian-limited-enforcement-report.pdf); [Farrer & Co](https://www.farrer.co.uk/news-and-insights/are-you-experian-ced--ico-criticised-by-tribunal-as-it-overturns-experian-enforcement-notice/)). Poland's regulator fined Bisnode **EUR 220,000** for using public-register data without notifying individuals, and ruled that a website privacy policy was not enough ([Data Protection People](https://dataprotectionpeople.com/resource-centre/company-fined-for-use-of-publicly-available-information/)). Applied to on-chain enrichment, an EU merchant would need several things. It would need a lawful basis, likely consent, for profiling someone's finances. The new use would have to pass a purpose-compatibility test against the original purpose of completing the order. It would need to send Article 14 notices naming "public blockchain" as a source, run a DPIA, and honor the absolute right to object to profiling for direct marketing. This applies standard GDPR principles. No regulator has yet ruled specifically on on-chain enrichment by a merchant.

Platform and payment rules add contractual limits on top. OpenAI asks, and in one account prohibits, merchants from signing ChatGPT buyers up for marketing email from their orders ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/customer-experience/openais-chatgpt-instant-checkout-the-dawn-of-conversational-commerce-cx/); [Azoma](https://www.azoma.ai/insights/chatgpt-instant-checkout-what-merchants-need-to-know)). No published OpenAI clause explicitly bans profiling, but the "specific transaction only" stance pulls against building one. Visa's Core Rules now cover agentic transactions and reportedly bar sharing the token outside the authorized chain ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=5c1b1a29-552a-4ec5-ac3d-d7bf0cc90793)). Stripe limits Stablecoin Payments to selling goods and services ([Stripe legal](https://stripe.com/legal/stablecoin-payments)). Travel Rule originator data, which would carry a real name, never reaches an ordinary merchant. In the EU, the providers that hold it are **barred from commercial use** of it ([AMLcube](https://www.amlcube.com/post/aml-update-an-overview-of-the-eu-transfer-of-funds-regulation-tfr)). The defense common among Web3 attribution vendors, Spindl's "just on-chain transactions, which are already public," together with its view that privacy is "up to the company using Spindl," leaves the risk with the merchant. After the EDPB's 2026 position, that defense is weak ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/05/21/web3-marketing-startup-spindl-launches-on-chain-ad-network); [Spindl](https://blog.spindl.xyz/p/the-spindl-take-on-web-3-privacy)). The researchers found no product called "Flovia" ([search result set](https://developer.paypal.com/limited-release/wlw/reporting-and-analytics/)). If it is a real company, its own documentation should be checked.

## What this means for Two Hands and a payer-analytics pivot

**Payer analytics built on OpenAI's wallet is not a market today.** The largest consumer agent wallet produces card tokens and a PAR, and OpenAI keeps the cross-merchant purchase graph for itself. Even if OpenAI adds stablecoins, the likely route runs through Stripe. There, Stripe-adjacent sources describe stablecoin support for SPTs as "coming soon" ([agenticplug.ai](https://agenticplug.ai/blog/what-is-stripe-link-wallet-for-ai-agents)), and Stripe's machine payments settle "into a business's existing balance, in their default currency" ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine)). Merchants would receive dollars and, at most, a sender address that Stripe itself reports. Building a business on getting more out of OpenAI's payment exhaust means competing with OpenAI's own data policy.

The rails where on-chain payer analytics does work, **x402 and MPP on Tempo paid from per-user agent wallets built on Privy, Coinbase Smart Wallet or Fireblocks** ([Privy x402](https://docs.privy.io/recipes/agent-integrations/x402.md); [Fireblocks](https://developers.fireblocks.com/docs/x402-concepts.md)), are the ones an MCP tool router actually touches. Agents paying per call for tools and APIs leave persistent, linkable addresses. That makes "who is this agent, what has it paid for, is it trustworthy" an answerable question, and a real opening for **agent identity and reputation**. Surveillance of human payers is a much weaker opening. The difference matters. Profiling the humans behind agent wallets runs straight into the EDPB guidelines, Article 14 notices and state profiling opt-outs. Verifying agents is what the networks and OpenAI already reward: Web Bot Auth, Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol with its consumer-recognition signal, and Mastercard Agentic Tokens that bind cardholder, agent and spending scope ([Mastercard Developers](https://developer.mastercard.com/merchant-cloud/documentation/tutorials-and-guides/agentic-commerce-guide/22/); [eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15192003-mastercard-agent-pay-vs-visa-trusted-agent-2026-compared)). A consent-based design, in which an agent presents signed credentials and an opt-in history, avoids the legal exposure that passive enrichment creates.

For an agent-payments router, the architecture follows from the table above: **be rail-agnostic and treat identity as a per-rail property**. A router that handles card tokens (ChatGPT Wallet, Stripe SPT, MPP's card path) alongside stablecoin rails (x402, MPP on Tempo) needs to know that the first group yields no on-chain identity, and that the second yields an identity whose meaning depends on custody. It must read UserOperation senders, not bundler addresses, label omnibus sources as platforms rather than people, and keep sanctions-screening data apart from any analytics. The weak spots in this assessment are real. Adoption numbers for x402 and MPP were not gathered, the share of stablecoin payments coming from omnibus versus embedded wallets is unknown, and OpenAI's merchant terms remain unread. Any pivot decision should start by measuring how much agent tool spending actually flows over x402 and MPP today.

## Conclusion

The question assumed "wallet" meant something on a blockchain. In agentic commerce in late 2026 it mostly does not. OpenAI chose card-network tokens precisely because they keep the payer's identity inside OpenAI and the networks, give merchants nothing to enrich, and preserve chargebacks. The result is an inversion. The most private agent payments are the mainstream card-token ones, and the most exposed are the crypto-native rails that marketed themselves as permissionless. Whatever traceable identity exists on those rails becomes regulated personal data the moment a merchant links it to an order.

For a founder, the defensible position is not to extract identity from payment exhaust. That is either impossible (card tokens) or legally fraught (stablecoin addresses). The defensible position is to become the layer agents use to prove who they are and what they are allowed to spend, across both rails. That is a product the platforms are asking for, and one where consent and accountability are features rather than liabilities.

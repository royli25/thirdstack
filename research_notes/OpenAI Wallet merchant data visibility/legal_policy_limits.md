# Legal, Contractual and Platform-Policy Limits on Merchants Profiling Payers from AI-Agent Checkouts, Wallets and Stablecoin Payments (as of 20 Nov 2026)

Research method note: WebFetch was blocked in this environment (developers.openai.com and www.edpb.europa.eu both failed DNS resolution under the session's network policy), so **every finding below comes from WebSearch result snippets/summaries, not from reading full primary documents**. Where the snippet is a secondary source (blog, vendor, aggregator), that is flagged. None of this is legal advice. The OpenAI ChatGPT Wallet launched only on 13 Nov 2026, a week before this research, so coverage is thin and mostly secondary.

## 1. OpenAI merchant terms / ACP / commerce policies: limits on buyer-data use

### Takeaway
I could not retrieve OpenAI's actual Instant Checkout / ChatGPT Wallet merchant agreement. Public statements and secondary coverage show three consistent restrictions: (a) data minimisation (only what is needed to complete the order is shared, with user permission); (b) OpenAI asks/prohibits merchants from signing ChatGPT buyers up for marketing email off the back of a ChatGPT order; (c) under ChatGPT Wallet, the merchant must not reuse or save the payment credential for a separate purchase. I found no public OpenAI text that explicitly bans "combining" or "profiling"; that silence is a gap, not a permission.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI's launch post (Instant Checkout + Agentic Commerce Protocol, Sept 2025): "only the information required to complete the order is shared with the merchant, with the user's permission." — [OpenAI: Buy it in ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/buy-it-in-chatgpt/)
- ACP flow: "ChatGPT collects buyer, fulfillment, and payment information from the user. The checkout session is rendered in the OpenAI UI, but the actual checkout state and payment processing occurs on the merchant's systems." (snippet) — [OpenAI developer docs: Key concepts](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/guides/key-concepts)
- Marketing email restriction (secondary, quoting OpenAI): "merchants use your order information to complete the order, but OpenAI asks merchants to not sign users up for marketing emails from their ChatGPT orders." — [CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/customer-experience/openais-chatgpt-instant-checkout-the-dawn-of-conversational-commerce-cx/); another secondary source describes it as an outright prohibition: "OpenAI prohibits merchants from signing up customers for marketing emails from ChatGPT orders." — [Azoma](https://www.azoma.ai/insights/chatgpt-instant-checkout-what-merchants-need-to-know). Note the two sources differ on "asks" vs "prohibits".
- Shopify-focused compliance guide advises separating order messages from marketing and obtaining a lawful basis (consent or soft opt-in depending on jurisdiction) before using an in-chat order email for marketing (vendor commentary). — [Consentmo](https://www.consentmo.com/blog-posts/chatgpt-checkout-agentic-commerce-what-shopify-merchants-need-to-know-and-whats-still-unclear)
- ChatGPT Wallet launched 13 Nov 2026 with American Express, Mastercard and Visa; initially US ChatGPT Pro users 18+, eligible US-issued credit cards only (no debit or virtual cards at launch). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/openai-hands-its-ai-agents-a-wallet-without-the-real-card-number-in-it/); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/330799/20261115/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-face-id-approval-sole-human-check-ai-agent-card-payments.htm); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/chatgpt-wallet)
- Under ChatGPT Wallet the merchant receives a network token, not the PAN, and "can use the Payment Account Reference (PAR) to associate that token with the underlying payment account." Secondary sources disagree on whether the token is single-use (OpenAI's post reportedly does not say clearly). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/openai-hands-its-ai-agents-a-wallet-without-the-real-card-number-in-it/); [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/chatgpt-wallet/)
- Wallet credential rule (quoting OpenAI's commerce partner playbook via secondary source): "The customer consents only to the specific transaction"; "Don't reuse or save that payment credential for a separate purchase." A checkout that auto-saves cards on every order would conflict. Recurring billing is permitted only with consent captured and processor merchant-initiated-transaction practices followed. — [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/ai/chatgpt-wallet-commerce-partner-playbook-merchants/); [The Keyword](https://www.thekeyword.co/news/openai-commerce-partner-playbook)
- OpenAI's Wallet help article reportedly says a merchant "may need your name, phone number, email, or billing or delivery address to complete checkout." OpenAI itself receives a tokenized credential plus limited card metadata (network, issuer, card type, last four). — [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/chatgpt-wallet); [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/chatgpt-wallet/)
- Under ChatGPT Wallet, purchases complete on merchant-owned sites/apps (agent "Dots" drives the merchant's own checkout), and fraud decisions are left to merchants; no Wallet-specific integration and no OpenAI transaction fee at launch. A recent overview says OpenAI is moving away from standalone Instant Checkout. — [Beri](https://www.beri.net/article/chatgpt-wallet-launch-dots-agent-checkout-merchant-network-token-web-bot-auth-cloudflare-akamai-allowlist-fraud-rules-product-feed); [Ecommerce Paradise](https://ecommerceparadise.com/chatgpt-wallet-ai-agent-checkout/); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/ai/chatgpt-wallet-commerce-partner-playbook-merchants/)
- OpenAI Merchant Feed Terms (dated 15 June 2026) cover product-feed content: OpenAI may use merchant content for a search index and to improve OpenAI services; if merchant content contains personal data (e.g., reviews) the parties comply with a data-protection Exhibit A. These are feed terms, not buyer-data terms. — [OpenAI Merchant Feed Terms](https://openai.com/policies/merchant-feed-terms-of-service/)
- Practitioner guidance (not a contract): share only minimum personal data, log agent-supplied user confirmations, and keep records of purpose-limited ACP data sharing for GDPR/CCPA. — [dropship.io](https://www.dropship.io/blog/openai-acp); [eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14845478-acp-agentic-commerce-protocol-explained)

### Inferences
- Because the Wallet flow lands on the merchant's own checkout, the merchant is likely the GDPR controller / CCPA "business" for the order data it collects, and its own privacy notice and lawful bases govern. OpenAI's contractual overlays (no marketing sign-ups off ChatGPT orders; no credential reuse) sit on top.
- The "no marketing sign-ups" and "consent only to the specific transaction" language signals a purpose-limited posture: using a ChatGPT-sourced payer identity to seed a persistent cross-context profile is at least in tension with the spirit of OpenAI's terms, even if no explicit "no profiling" clause has been published.
- The PAR is explicitly offered to merchants to link a Wallet token to the underlying account, which suggests OpenAI/networks contemplate merchants recognising returning payers (e.g., for fraud and loyalty); this is not the same as permission to enrich with third-party or on-chain data.
- ChatGPT Wallet is card-token based at launch; I found no evidence of an OpenAI stablecoin/crypto wallet, so the "link wallet address to on-chain history" question does not arise from OpenAI Wallet payments themselves as of Nov 2026.

### Gaps
- Full text of OpenAI's Instant Checkout merchant agreement, ChatGPT Wallet merchant terms/commerce partner playbook, and Wallet Privacy Notice: not retrieved (WebFetch blocked). Specific clauses on retention periods, onward sharing, combination with other data, and profiling remain unverified.
- The ACP specification (agenticcommerce.dev) was not read; I found no source stating ACP itself imposes binding retention or deletion duties (it is a protocol; obligations are likely in the commercial agreement).
- Whether OpenAI passes a stable buyer identifier (beyond name/email/address) to merchants is unknown.

## 2. Stripe (Shared Payment Tokens, Link, stablecoins) and card-network rules (Visa, Mastercard)

### Takeaway
Stripe's SPTs are scoped, expiring references to a payment method granted to a single seller; their use is governed by separate agent/seller terms I could not retrieve. Stripe's stablecoin charge objects expose the buyer's wallet address (needed for refunds), and Stripe's Stablecoin Payments terms limit use to selling goods/services. Visa's Core Rules now include agentic-transaction provisions (token use, no sharing outside the authorised chain); Mastercard's public rules are silent on agentic specifics. I found no network rule text directly addressing using tokens/PAR for marketing tracking.

### Cited Findings
- An SPT is "a limited reference to a payment method" stored in the agent's Stripe account, with usage limits and expiration windows, that never reveals PANs; each SPT is granted to one seller account only; sellers receive webhooks on usage/deactivation; "by using SPTs, you agree to the terms of service" (linked separately). — [Stripe docs: Shared payment tokens](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/concepts/shared-payment-tokens)
- Sellers are told to "review the agent terms and request onboarding for the agent in the Dashboard" before selling through an agent. — [Stripe docs: custom integration](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/sellers/custom)
- Stripe stablecoin (deposit mode, private preview) charge records include `payment_method_details.crypto` with transaction hash, buyer wallet address, network and token currency; refunds go back to the originating wallet. — [Stripe docs: Deposit mode stablecoin payments](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/deposit-mode-stablecoin-payments); [eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15083174-stripe-stablecoin-payments-2026)
- Stripe Stablecoin Payments terms: "You may only use Stablecoin Payments to sell your goods and services to Customers"; Stripe disclaims liability for incorrect wallet addresses supplied for refunds. — [Stripe legal: Stablecoin Payments](https://stripe.com/legal/stablecoin-payments)
- Stripe guidance says businesses accepting stablecoins should screen wallet addresses against sanctions lists, monitor flows and keep auditable records. — [Stripe: accepting stablecoin payments](https://stripe.com/resources/more/accepting-stablecoin-payments)
- Visa Core Rules now contain agentic-transaction provisions requiring identity verification per Visa Intelligent Commerce specs and use of the provisioned token, and reportedly bar sharing the token with any party outside the authorised chain; Mastercard's public Transaction Processing Rules "remain silent on agentic terminology" (law-firm analysis). — [Lexology: How Network Rules Already Govern Agentic Commerce](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=5c1b1a29-552a-4ec5-ac3d-d7bf0cc90793)
- Mastercard Agentic Tokens are described as MDES tokens extended with agent, consent and limit data; Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol and Mastercard's Web Bot Auth let merchants verify agents (vendor commentary). — [Shatale](https://shatale.com/blog/mastercard-agent-pay-visa-intelligent-commerce); [agentspay.ai](https://agentspay.ai/blog/agentic-tokens-explained)
- PAR: a non-sensitive EMVCo identifier linking tokens to the underlying account; industry sources describe its use for loyalty and analytics; Visa's PAR Inquiry API lets merchants/acquirers retrieve the PAR for a PAN or token; PCI SSC says PAR alone is not PCI account data. — [Finextra/EMVCo](https://finextra.com/pressarticle/63726/emvco-updates-payment-tokenisation-specification-to-introduce-payment-account-reference); [Visa developer: PAR Inquiry](https://developer.visa.com/capabilities/visa-par-inquiry); [PCI SSC FAQ 1374](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/faqs/1374/)

### Inferences
- Network/processor rules mainly constrain the **credential** (no storage, reuse, or sharing outside the authorised chain; PCI scope), not downstream marketing use of the payer's identity. Marketing/profiling limits come mainly from privacy law and the agent platform's (OpenAI's) terms.
- PAR is a persistent cross-token account identifier and would likely be "personal data" (GDPR) / a "unique identifier" (CCPA) in the merchant's hands; using it to build cross-channel profiles brings it under privacy-law profiling and notice obligations even though it is out of PCI scope.
- For stablecoin payments via Stripe, the merchant lawfully receives the payer's wallet address for refunds/sanctions screening; reusing it to pull public on-chain history for marketing is a new purpose that must pass GDPR purpose-compatibility (Art. 6(4)) or get a separate lawful basis (background legal knowledge; not verified against a source in this session).

### Gaps
- Stripe's agentic-commerce/SPT agent terms, Link terms and the data-use sections of the Stripe Services Agreement were not retrieved; I cannot confirm any explicit Stripe restriction on seller use of agent-checkout customer data.
- Visa/Mastercard rule text on permitted merchant uses of tokens/PAR for loyalty/marketing was not found; only secondary summaries.

## 3. Privacy law: is a wallet address personal data? Profiling, purpose limitation, enrichment from public sources

### Takeaway
EU: EDPB Guidelines 02/2025 (final v2.0 adopted 7 July 2026) state that a wallet address/public key qualifies as personal data when it can be associated with an identifiable person, and the EDPB rejected consultation requests to carve wallet addresses out. A merchant that links an address to a named customer's order has, by definition, made it identifiable, so the address and any on-chain history linked to it become personal data in the merchant's hands. US: no CPPA/AG ruling squarely on wallet addresses, but the CCPA definition (unique/online identifiers, info "reasonably capable of being associated") and the AG's 2022 opinion that inferences drawn from public information are still personal information point the same way. Enrichment from public sources triggers notice duties (GDPR Art. 14), as the ICO/Experian and Polish Bisnode cases show.

### Cited Findings
- EDPB adopted the final version of Guidelines 02/2025 on processing personal data through blockchain technologies (v2.0, 7 July 2026); consultation ran 14 April - 9 June 2025. — [EDPB news release](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-sheds-light-on-anonymisation-and-web-scraping-for-generative-ai-and-adopts-final-version_en); [EDPB Guidelines v2 PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_202502_blockchain_v2_en.pdf)
- Glossary (snippet): "A wallet address/account/public key is an identifier linked to a cryptographic key pair in a blockchain network. This identifier qualifies as personal data under Article 4 GDPR when it can be associated with an identified or identifiable natural person." — [EDPB Guidelines v2 PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_202502_blockchain_v2_en.pdf)
- "On-chain metadata, including transaction identifiers, wallet addresses, event logs, receipts, state transitions and smart contract storage and related traces, may constitute personal data when they enable direct or indirect identification of a natural person." (snippet) — [EDPB Guidelines v2 PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_202502_blockchain_v2_en.pdf)
- EDPB consultation report: suggestions "to exclude wallet addresses or transaction-level identifiers from the notion of personal data, irrespective of their potential to identify or single out individuals" were not adopted. — [EDPB public consultation report](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpbreport_publicconsultaton_guidelines_blockchain_202502_en.pdf)
- Commentary: encrypted/hashed data stays personal data when linkable via external means such as a wallet address; unsalted hashes insufficient on a public chain; EDPB advises against storing personal data on-chain. — [Harris Kyriakides](https://www.harriskyriakides.law/insights/news/edpb-blockchain-guidelines-gdpr-compliance/); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320026/20260709/eu-finalizes-gdpr-blockchain-rules-encrypted-data-still-personal-data.htm); [blockchain-gdpr.org (Erbguth)](https://blockchain-gdpr.org/edpb-guidelines-v2-changes.html); [Dudkowiak](https://www.dudkowiak.com/blog/blockchain-and-the-gdpr-the-edpbs-final-guidelines-for-the-fintech-and-crypto-sectors)
- CCPA: personal information includes information "reasonably capable of being associated with" a consumer and identifiers such as "unique personal identifier, online identifier"; a unique personal identifier is a persistent identifier that can recognise a consumer/device across services. — [CA Lawyers Association](https://calawyers.org/privacy-law/what-is-personal-information-under-the-california-consumer-privacy-act/); [Cal. Civ. Code 1798.140 text](https://www.consumerprivacyact.com/section-1798-140-definitions/)
- Law-firm commentary: each blockchain address is unique, ENS-linked wallets often identify the person, and CCPA activity-data language is "arguably broad enough to capture transaction activity on the blockchain"; Davis Polk: better practice treats public keys as tokenised personal information rather than anonymised data. (Dated sources.) — [JD Supra: NFTs privacy issues](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/nfts-privacy-issues-for-consideration-7804114/); [Davis Polk paper](https://www.davispolk.com/sites/default/files/blockchain_technology_data_privacy_issues_and_potential_mitigation_strategies_w-021-8235.pdf)
- CPRA "publicly available" exception covers information a business reasonably believes was lawfully made available to the general public by the consumer; but the California AG's 2022 CCPA opinion held "inferences drawn entirely from publicly available information still need to be disclosed" because the inference is itself personal information. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-ag-issues-decision-on-disclosure-of-inference-data-in-response-to-ccpa-access-requests); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/california-ags-first-ccpa-opinion-takes-a-broad-view-of-the-right-to-access-inferences.md); [Byte Back: CPRA/CPA/VCDPA publicly available info](https://www.bytebacklaw.com/2022/01/how-do-the-cpra-cpa-vcdpa-treat-publicly-available-information/)
- Counterpoint: some sources say an address alone does not reveal identity; a Korean-law analysis says a wallet address alone is not personal information but becomes so when combined with other data (e.g., IP). — [LinkedIn / Tiger Research](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/your-wallet-address-personal-information-tiger-research-inc-kblac)
- Enrichment enforcement (UK, pre-dates on-chain): ICO found Experian engaged in "invisible processing" of data from publicly available sources without adequate transparency and ordered Article 14 notices; the Tribunal (Feb 2023) overturned much of the notice but rejected Experian's "disproportionate effort" argument under Art. 14. — [ICO Experian enforcement report](https://ico.org.uk/media2/migrated/2618467/experian-limited-enforcement-report.pdf); [Farrer & Co](https://www.farrer.co.uk/news-and-insights/are-you-experian-ced--ico-criticised-by-tribunal-as-it-overturns-experian-enforcement-notice/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102i8l8/a-sigh-of-relief-for-data-brokers-tribunal-overturns-most-of-icos-experian-en/)
- Poland: Warsaw administrative court upheld UODO's EUR 220,000 fine on Bisnode for processing public-register data without individually notifying data subjects; a website privacy policy was insufficient. — [Data Protection People](https://dataprotectionpeople.com/resource-centre/company-fined-for-use-of-publicly-available-information/)

### Inferences
- For a merchant like Nike, the moment a wallet address is attached to an order (name, email, shipping address) it is unambiguously personal data under the EDPB test, and so is every on-chain transaction pulled in for that address. "It's public on-chain" is not a GDPR exemption; publicness is only a factor in the legitimate-interests balance.
- Pulling a payer's full on-chain history (other merchants, DeFi, balances, counterparties) is new-source collection: it requires (i) a lawful basis (legitimate interests is hard to sustain for extensive financial-behaviour profiling of retail customers; consent is the safer route), (ii) passing Art. 6(4) purpose compatibility versus the original "complete the order" purpose, (iii) Art. 14 notice to each data subject within one month, (iv) a DPIA (systematic evaluation/profiling, financial data, innovative tech are typical DPIA triggers), and (v) honouring the Art. 21 absolute right to object to direct-marketing profiling. This is background GDPR knowledge applied to the facts, not a specific regulator statement on on-chain enrichment.
- On-chain history can reveal special-category inferences (donations, gambling, health-related purchases) and wealth, raising Art. 9 and fairness risks; a profile used for differential pricing could also engage Art. 22 if solely automated with significant effects.
- In the US, the CCPA/CPRA would treat the linked address and inferences as personal information (AG opinion on inferences), requiring notice at collection of the categories/sources ("public blockchain") and purposes, and likely access/deletion rights over the derived profile. Other state laws (CO, VA, CT, etc.) contain profiling opt-out rights for decisions with legal/similarly significant effects and targeted-advertising opt-outs (background knowledge; not verified in this session).
- A "Flovia-style" snippet that reads a connected wallet from the browser (e.g., an injected provider) may also engage ePrivacy Art. 5(3) (access to information on terminal equipment) in the EU, per EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 on its technical scope, independently of GDPR (background knowledge; not verified in this session).

### Gaps
- I could not read the full EDPB v2 text, so specific paragraph numbers, and whether it addresses third parties that read/analyse public chain data (vs. those that write to chains), are unverified.
- No regulator guidance, enforcement action or court decision found (EU, UK or US) that specifically addresses a merchant or analytics firm enriching customer records with on-chain data.
- No California CPPA guidance specifically on wallet addresses found.
- ICO-specific blockchain guidance not searched.

## 4. Travel Rule / AML (FinCEN, MiCA/TFR): what originator data reaches merchants, and can it be used for marketing?

### Takeaway
Travel Rule data flows between regulated intermediaries (CASPs/VASPs, PSPs, and under GENIUS Act rulemaking, permitted stablecoin issuers), not to an ordinary merchant receiving funds into its own wallet. In the EU, TFR data may be used only for AML/CFT and providers are barred from commercial use of it. A merchant generally does not receive Travel Rule originator data unless its own payment processor/CASP shares it, and that processor is itself barred from commercial use.

### Cited Findings
- EU TFR (Reg. (EU) 2023/1113): information obtained under the travel rule "should only be used for the purposes of preventing money laundering and terrorist financing"; "PSPs, CASPs and intermediaries are not allowed to use that information for commercial purposes"; transmission must comply with GDPR. (Secondary compliance-vendor summary.) — [AMLcube](https://www.amlcube.com/post/aml-update-an-overview-of-the-eu-transfer-of-funds-regulation-tfr)
- TFR has no de minimis threshold for crypto-asset transfers between CASPs. — [AMLcube](https://www.amlcube.com/post/aml-update-an-overview-of-the-eu-transfer-of-funds-regulation-tfr)
- Commentary: AML-required processing rests on GDPR Art. 6(1)(c) legal obligation, while marketing and non-AML data follow standard rules. — [Crypto Law Index](https://cryptolawindex.com/knowledge/eu-travel-rule-tfr-implementation-by-member-state-2026/)
- Commentary on the Travel Rule's data-protection liability (Malta, Sept 2026). — [MK Fintech Partners](https://mkfintechpartners.com/2026/09/02/the-travel-rule-an-effective-aml-cft-tool-or-an-expensive-data-protection-liability/)
- US: FinCEN and OFAC proposed (April 2026) AML/sanctions program rules for permitted payment stablecoin issuers (PPSIs) under the GENIUS Act, including Recordkeeping and Travel Rule compliance for transfers of USD 3,000 or more; CIP to come in a separate rulemaking; comments due 9 June 2026. FinCEN's 2019 guidance (FIN-2019-G001) already applied the Travel Rule to convertible virtual currency transmittals. — [FinCEN PPSI NPRM fact sheet](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-04/FactSheet-PPSI-program-NPRM.pdf); [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260507-treasury-proposes-new-aml-cft-and-sanctions-obligations); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/04/treasury-proposes-aml-cft-and-sanctions-compliance-rules-for-stablecoin-issuers-under-the-genius-act); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/04/implementing-the-genius-act-fincen-and-ofac-propose)
- One law-firm advisory cites a Federal Register date of 19 Sept 2026 that conflicts with the April 2026 timeline in other sources (unresolved). — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/ja-JP/Insights/2026/04/FinCEN-and-OFAC-Propose-Rule-for-Payment-Stablecoin-Issuers-to-Implement-GENIUS-Act)

### Inferences
- A merchant paid directly on-chain (customer's self-hosted wallet to merchant's wallet) receives only the on-chain data (address, amount, tx hash), not Travel Rule originator name/account data. If paid via a custodial exchange to a merchant's CASP/processor, originator data goes to the processor for AML; the TFR commercial-use ban means it should not be passed on or used for marketing.
- In the US, the Travel Rule is a recordkeeping/transmittal obligation on financial institutions; a merchant buying/selling its own goods for stablecoin is generally not a money transmitter (background knowledge; not confirmed by a source in this session). Even where a US processor holds Travel Rule data, using it for marketing would raise GLBA (non-public personal information) and privacy-notice issues for the processor (background; unverified).
- Sanctions screening of the payer's address is permitted and even advised (see Stripe guidance in section 2), but screening data collected for compliance should be kept separate from marketing datasets.

### Gaps
- Primary text of TFR Art. 25-26 (data protection / purpose limitation) and EBA Travel Rule guidelines not retrieved.
- Whether the final GENIUS Act Travel Rule treats merchants or unhosted-wallet transfers differently: not found.
- MiCA-specific provisions on CASP data reuse for marketing: not searched.

## 5. How comparable wallet-analytics businesses handle compliance (Flovia, Nansen, Arkham, Spindl, Cookie3, Addressable, Blockchain-ads)

### Takeaway
I found no evidence that "Flovia" exists as a product (no search results). Comparable Web3 attribution/intelligence vendors publicly rely on the argument that on-chain data is "public" and "pseudonymous, so no personal data", which conflicts with the EDPB's 2026 position; at least one (Spindl) pushes privacy decisions onto its customers. Arkham's deanonymisation bounty model drew privacy and GDPR criticism. No GDPR enforcement against any of these vendors was found.

### Cited Findings
- "Flovia": search returned no product, company or wallet of that name. — [WebSearch result set, no matching hits; nearest results were unrelated PayPal/ACI wallet analytics pages](https://developer.paypal.com/limited-release/wlw/reporting-and-analytics/)
- Spindl's CEO: "We're not using weird, sketchy Web2 data," such as browsing history or personal information, "just on-chain transactions, which are already public." — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/05/21/web3-marketing-startup-spindl-launches-on-chain-ad-network)
- Spindl's blog describes measurement tied to pseudonymous wallet activity ("no personal data involved") and says it is "up to the company using Spindl to decide on their approach to privacy." — [Spindl blog: The Spindl take on Web3 privacy](https://blog.spindl.xyz/p/the-spindl-take-on-web-3-privacy)
- Third-party profile says Spindl links off-chain touchpoints to on-chain events via fingerprinting, UTM-style tagging and signed wallet messages (unverified secondary source). — [formo.so: Web3 attribution vendors](https://formo.so/blog/web3-marketing-analytics-attribution-vendors)
- Cookie3 maps influencer/social campaigns to on-chain wallet activity ("KOL Intelligence"); Addressable does wallet-based advertising (no privacy details found). — [web3trackers: Spindl vs Cookie3](https://www.web3trackers.com/compare/spindl-vs-cookie3); [formo.so: top Web3 analytics platforms](https://formo.so/blog/top-web3-analytics-platforms-marketing)
- Arkham: "Deanonymization is destiny" (white paper); its Intel Exchange paid bounties for identifying wallet owners, drawing privacy-advocate criticism; one article cites concerns about false labels and potential GDPR violations (allegation, no legal analysis); Arkham says it only provides publicly available information and focuses on sanctioned parties and fraudsters; label accuracy varies. — [Wikipedia: Arkham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkham_(cryptocurrency_exchange)); [DailyCoin](https://dailycoin.com/arkham-crypto-is-dox-to-earn-threat-to-cryptocurrency/) (low-quality source); [VisibleMagic](https://visiblemagic.com/news/blockchain-security); [OSINTBench review](https://osintbench.com/tools/arkham-intel/)
- Conflicting reports on Arkham's Intel Exchange status (one aggregator says shut down Dec 2025; Wikipedia mentions an Enterprise tier launch Oct 2026); unverified. — [rfp.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/crypto/digital-assets-nfts/crypto-data-analytics-market-risk/arkham-intelligence); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkham_(cryptocurrency_exchange))

### Inferences
- The industry's "public + pseudonymous = no personal data" defence is weak in the EU after EDPB Guidelines 02/2025 v2.0, and collapses entirely for a merchant that holds the payer's name/email next to the address. A Nike-type merchant deploying such a snippet would be the controller (or joint controller with the vendor, per CJEU Fashion ID logic for embedded third-party code; background knowledge) and could not rely on the vendor's framing.
- Vendors pushing privacy decisions to customers (Spindl) means compliance risk sits with the merchant: it would need a DPA with the vendor, a lawful basis (likely consent for profiling/ad targeting), ePrivacy consent for any client-side reading/fingerprinting, Art. 14 notices for on-chain sources, and US notice/opt-out mechanisms for "sharing" for cross-context behavioural advertising under CPRA if the vendor uses the data across clients.
- Nansen-style labelling and Arkham-style deanonymisation are mostly aimed at entities, funds and investigations; their compliance posture is not a safe model for a consumer brand profiling retail customers.
